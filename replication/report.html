IRW Tests of the Spacing Law

Chituc's stretched-exponential page finds rating categories that widen geometrically in log-stimulus space, r ≈ 1.17–1.41. We ran his battery on four IRW datasets — including 604,265 individual word ratings, the level his own word-familiarity result cannot reach.

01

The battery reproduces his published result.

Re-run on the Glasgow Norms it returns r = 1.47 (1.36 before trimming sparse cells) against his 1.409, and recovers a known r without bias on simulated data.

02

At the item-mean level, the signature replicates.

23k English words rated for pronunciation difficulty against SUBTLEX frequency give r = 1.39; Arabic age-of-acquisition norms 1.21; creative-quality ratings 1.19. Equidistant spacing loses everywhere.

03

At the individual-rating level, it disappears.

The same words and the same raters, un-averaged: r = 0.97, and a median of 1.01 across 366 individual raters. A null model with no widening at all, averaged and rounded the way item norms are, manufactures r = 1.29 of the observed 1.39.

04

And the widening sits at the wrong end of the scale.

In all three IRW batteries the rated attribute runs against frequency. The widening tracks the high-frequency end of the stimulus every time — which is the bottom of the scale as administered, not the top.

What was run

The battery, and what it had to beat

Chituc's observable claim is that an ordinal rating, treated as a categorization of a stimulus Φ measured in log-Φ space, has category boundaries whose widths grow by a constant factor r > 1 per step. We implemented both halves of his test from the page:

  • The mean-function race. OLS/NLS of log Φ on the rating S: stretched exponential log Φ = c + A·eβS against quadratic, linear-in-S (Fechner), power law, and free category means. AIC on observations, R² on the category means.
  • The threshold tournament. An anchored graded-response model (ordinal logistic on standardized log Φ) fit under three threshold families — free (K parameters), equidistant (3), and geometric widths (4). r is read off the geometric family, which is the estimate his page treats as authoritative.

Two checks before trusting any of it. On simulated data the estimator returns 1.008, 1.237 and 1.403 for true r of 1.00, 1.24 and 1.41 — no bias, and a clean null. And re-run on his own word-familiarity battery (Glasgow FAM item means × SUBTLEX Zipf, 5,515 words after the join) it returns r = 1.474, or 1.363 if sparse categories are kept, against his published 1.409. That row is the control throughout, shaded below.

One orientation convention, used throughout. The law is directional: widths that grow toward the top of the scale shrink toward the bottom, so a reversal turns r into 1/r. Every battery here is stated with the rating running the same way as frequency, so the numbers are comparable to the Glasgow control. Where the IRW variable runs the other way it is reversed and renamed — "ease" for 7 − difficulty, "earliness" for 8 − age of acquisition, "conventionality" for 6 − creative quality. Section 4 is about what that reversal costs.

Results

Five batteries

All five use a frequency count as the stimulus. The IRW join to SUBTLEX-US matched 22,791 of the 23,282 word strings in spelling2pronounce_edwards2023 (97.9%); the other two carry their frequency in-table.

Finding 1

The aggregation step manufactures most of it

spelling2pronounce_edwards2023 is the only battery here with individual responses at scale: 629,815 ratings of 23,282 word strings by 2,515 raters, one item ("difficulty"). That makes it the first chance to ask whether the spacing law is a fact about raters or a fact about item norms — the caveat Chituc's page states about its own word-familiarity result, which is aggregate-only.

Analyzed his way — mean over raters, rounded to an integer, one row per word — it gives r = 1.388. Analyzed as 604,265 separate ratings it gives r = 0.972. Fit rater by rater on the 400 raters sampled from the 2,408 who rated at least 200 words, the median is 1.008, the interquartile range [0.889, 1.111], and 52.7% of raters land above 1 — a coin flip.

A generating model with no widening in it at all, averaged over raters and rounded the way item norms are, produces r = 1.285. The observed value is 1.388.

That is the direct test. We took the fitted individual-level model — free thresholds, r = 0.972, no widening — added a word random effect tuned until simulated per-word means matched the real dispersion (σ = 0.767, giving an SD of 0.684 against the observed 0.683), simulated each word's real number of raters, then averaged and rounded exactly as the aggregate battery does. Across six replicates the manufactured r was 1.275–1.300. Roughly 77% of the observed widening, on a log scale, is made by the aggregation step alone.

The same test run on the Glasgow Norms comes out the other way, and this matters for how the finding should be read. A strictly Fechnerian generating model — an individual familiarity rating linear in Zipf plus noise, calibrated to Glasgow's own slope (0.647 per Zipf unit), its residual between-word SD (0.713), its per-word rating SDs (median 1.51) and its per-word N (median 33) — yields only r = 1.087 against the observed 1.474. Chituc's word-familiarity result survives its aggregation null; the IRW word-difficulty result does not. The honest conclusion is not that the law is an artifact, but that the aggregate unit of analysis needs a null model attached, and that IRW is where the individual-level data to build one lives.

Finding 2

The widening follows the stimulus, not the scale

In the Glasgow battery the top of the rating scale and the high-frequency end of the stimulus are the same place: familiar words are frequent words. That coincidence is what makes the result hard to interpret — it cannot separate "the report scale compresses at its top" (the psychometric law S = k·log ψ the page derives the form from) from "log frequency is spaced this way relative to any judgment correlated with it".

All three IRW batteries break that coincidence, because in each of them the rated attribute runs against frequency:

  • Pronunciation difficulty (spelling2pronounce, item-level correlation with Zipf = −0.354): rarer words are harder.
  • Age of acquisition (kalimahnorms_alzahrani_2025, Arabic, K = 8, frequency in-table): rarer words are learned later.
  • Creative quality (Forthmann-2024-creative_quality, itemcov_frequency on the idea): rarer ideas score higher.

Three for three, the widening lands at the high-frequency end of the stimulus — which is the bottom of every one of those scales as it was actually administered. Categories are widest where words are easiest, learned earliest, and ideas most conventional. On the report-scale reading, the widening should have followed the top of the response scale in each case, and it does not.

A defender can answer that raters mentally rate "ease" rather than difficulty, and the reversal is bookkeeping. That answer is harder to run three times: "creative quality" is unambiguously the positively-worded pole of its scale. The more parsimonious reading is a compression in the frequency-to-judgment mapping itself — very frequent words are all easy, all early, all conventional, so boundaries spread out at that end regardless of what is being judged.

The anchor control does not settle it here

Chituc's "range, not frequency" section shows the log-magnitude anchor beating a rank/CDF anchor by ΔAIC ≈ 10 on the IGN data. Run on word frequency the result reverses and is small: the rank anchor wins by 6.7 AIC on Glasgow and 70.2 on the individual spelling data, and the fitted r is essentially identical under both (1.363 vs 1.350; 0.9715 vs 0.9713). That is a statement about Zipf distributions, not about the law — log frequency and the normal quantile of frequency rank are close to linearly related in these corpora, so the test has almost no power on word data. It should not be read either way.

Where this leaves it

What we'd say to Vlad

  • The estimator and the finding both hold up under re-implementation. An independent build of the battery, from the page's prose, reproduces the published word-familiarity r to within the trimming rule, and reproduces the whole ordering of the mean-function race.
  • The aggregate-only caveat now has a number on it. In the one corpus where individual ratings exist at scale, average-then-round generates r = 1.29 out of nothing. Glasgow's own null only reaches 1.09, so its result survives — but every future aggregate battery should report the null alongside the estimate, because the size of the artifact depends on rater count, within-word SD, and where the item means sit relative to the scale bounds.
  • Individual-level replication is the open question. 604,265 single ratings of 23k words give r = 0.97 and a per-rater median of 1.01. That is one construct on one 6-point scale with a weak anchor (|ρ| = 0.35), so it is not decisive against the law. It is decisive that the question is answerable, and that the answer is not automatic.
  • The next test is a familiarity rating with individual responses. emoji_scheffler_2024 has familiarity ratings of 107 emoji on a 0–100 scale (K = 101) by 153 raters, and emoji frequency is objectively countable; famous_melodies has familiarity and age-of-acquisition for 109 melodies from 397 raters on a 9-point scale. Either would put the exact Glasgow construct at the individual level, and the two together span K = 9 → 101 — the sweep the page predicts should drive r → 1. Both need an external exposure count assembled first.

Caveats we'd want stated with the numbers

  • r moves with the sparse-cell rule: the Glasgow control is 1.474 under the 0.5% rule and 1.363 keeping all categories. Every number here uses the one rule, applied to every row.
  • Frequency is the stimulus only for the Glasgow battery. For difficulty, age of acquisition and creative quality it is a correlate, and a weak one — which limits how much a negative result in those three can carry on its own.
  • The threshold likelihood is evaluated on x binned into 600 equal-count cells for speed. On the simulation check this changes recovered r in the fourth decimal.
  • The Arabic norms code 0 as a distinct response for both age of acquisition and concreteness with a mean frequency far off the trend, which reads as a missing-data code; it is dropped. Keeping it moves that battery's r substantially.
