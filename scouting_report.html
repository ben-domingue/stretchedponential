IRW Candidates for the Spacing Law

Which datasets in the Item Response Warehouse can carry the geometric-category-width battery from Chituc’s stretched-exponential page — and, more to the point, which ones test something the three existing domains structurally cannot.

The filter

Three ingredients, all at once

Ingredient
An ordinal rating

A reported score S with K ≥ 5 categories. IRW has these in abundance — 1,343 tables at K = 5, 512 at K = 7, and a tail running out to K = 1,152.

Binding constraint
An objective Φ

A ratio-scaled magnitude attached to the thing being rated. This is what almost no IRW table has, and it is what decides the whole shortlist.

Ingredient
Enough objects

Enough distinct rated objects to estimate GRM thresholds anchored on log Φ, and enough raters per object to fit r individually.

In a normal IRW table item is a questionnaire item and id is a person — neither carries an objective magnitude, so ingredient 2 fails and the table is out. The tables that survive are the ones where id is a stimulus and there is a rater column. There are 29 of those in the warehouse. A second, partly overlapping pool is the 41 tables carrying itemcov_* fields, a handful of which already hold a usable Φ in the table itself.

That is the whole search space. Everything below comes out of it.

This is the one that pays for the trip. The actual argument

What the IRW adds that a fourth dataset wouldn’t

Four of these are things the existing three domains cannot deliver for structural reasons, not for want of effort.

Individual-level ratings, at scale

Retires the page’s own aggregate-level caveat on the word-frequency domain. spelling2pronounce alone yields 2,515 per-rater estimates of r, against fourteen historical observers on the stellar side and item means on the Glasgow side.

A sweep across K

β = 1/(kγ) predicts r should fall toward 1 as the scale gets finer. Frequency-anchored tables sit at K = 6 (spelling2pronounce), K = 9 (famous_melodies) and K = 101 (emoji_scheffler) with stimulus type roughly held fixed. That is a sharp quantitative prediction no single dataset can reach.

Same stimuli, many dimensions

fractals_rating carries 7 rating dimensions, Ellipse 8, famous_melodies and emoji_scheffler 5 each — all on identical objects. If r is a fact about report scales it should hold across dimensions; if it tracks the construct it should not. Directly separable here.

Range and frequency controls, across corpora

Different tables present raters with very different stimulus distributions. Under the range reading r should follow the presented range and ignore the frequency profile — a between-dataset version of the Parducci control that a single corpus can only approximate.

Checked and ruled out

Dead ends, so nobody re-walks them

concretewords
The item column holds Qualtrics response hashes, not the words. Unusable as it stands.
klatt_2016_speed_estimation
Only two objective speeds in the design, 50 and 55 km/h. No stimulus range to fit thresholds against.
mentalrotation_wolf_2024
Has itemcov_angle, but four levels only and binary responses.
roar_lexical · spalex_aguasvivas_2020
Words with objective frequency, but the response is binary accuracy rather than a rating. Wrong shape.
amatus_cipora_2024_arithmetic · himmelstein-*
Same problem: objective item properties, dichotomous responses.
allen_2025_delaydiscount
Item text carries objective amounts and delays, but the response is a binary choice.
de_vries_2022_hexaco_other · socialstereotype_hughes_2025_* · hpwt_bayona_2025_jobperformance
Most of the 29 rater-structured tables are people rating people. No objective Φ is available for the rated object.
Recommended first move

Run the battery on spelling2pronounce × SUBTLEX

It is the cleanest join in the warehouse, it is individual-level, and it patches the one caveat the page flags against its own word-frequency domain. Forthmann-2024-creative_quality and det_naismith_2023 follow as near-free second and third runs, since Φ is already sitting in those tables.

The obvious next step before committing: pull spelling2pronounce and check what fraction of the 23,282 word strings match a SUBTLEX entry.
